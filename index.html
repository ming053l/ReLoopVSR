ReLoopVSR: One-Step Recurrent Autoregressive Diffusion Refinement for Streaming Video Super-Resolution

Chia-Ming Lee1,2, Ming-Ching Chang1, Xin Li1, Yu-Lun Liu2, Chih-Chung Hsu2

1University at Albany, SUNY    2National Yang Ming Chiao Tung University

ReLoopVSR restores a video stream without future frames, in one denoising step per frame. It aligns and re-noises the previous restoration, refines it with the current low-resolution frame, and restarts wherever errors are predicted or the scene cuts. It leads in perceptual quality with far lower memory and latency, and ReLoopVSR-fast exceeds 240 FPS at 720p.

Method

Recurrent autoregressive refinement

Streaming VSR reconstructs high-resolution frames as low-resolution observations arrive, without future frames and within a bounded per-frame budget. After motion compensation, previously restored content already estimates the current frame, so ReLoopVSR corrects that estimate instead of generating each frame from noise. At frame k, the output depends only on the current low-resolution input and a fixed-size recurrent state that caches latents, RGB outputs and decoder features.

  • The previous denoised latent is aligned to the current frame, re-noised so that inherited detail can be revised, and refined in one denoising step conditioned on the current observation. Repeated updates to temporally corresponding content form a spatiotemporal momentum, which extends effective computational depth with fixed parameters.
  • However, alignment errors, disocclusions, lighting changes and scene cuts invalidate inherited content and cause error spiking. Local and global reliability control therefore truncates this momentum on the fly, restarting unreliable tiles from the current LR observation and resetting the full state at global changes.
  • Because each output becomes the starting point of the next frame, the model is trained on its own streaming rollouts and then with perceptual temporal supervision, so that it learns to correct inherited errors.
01

Recurrent refinement in latent space

ReLoopVSR uses the previous restoration as an initial estimate. Optical flow between the bicubic ×4-upsampled current and previous LR inputs aligns the cached denoised latent to frame k. The aligned latent is re-noised to an intermediate level and refined in a single denoising step, and the refined latent is cached as the state for the next frame.

  • As in SDEdit and ResShift, re-noising lets the current observation revise inherited detail. Stream-DiffVSR, by contrast, starts each four-step trajectory from noise and uses the previous restoration only as a condition.
  • The design is inspired by looped Transformers, which reuse shared parameters across iterations to increase effective depth in a compact model. Here the iterations run across frames, at one denoising step per frame.
  • On YouHQ40 and REDS4, starting from encoded LR instead of the propagated latent leaves spatial scores nearly unchanged but raises tLP by 12%, and removing the whole recurrent state raises tLP most, from 0.596 to 1.093.
02

Local and global reliability control

In consistent scenes the propagated restoration strengthens a compact model, but local inconsistencies such as moving people and global ones such as lighting changes and scene cuts cause error spiking when they are propagated. A lightweight Dynamic Estimation Network (DEN) therefore predicts the restoration error of the aligned latent from the current LR input, the warped previous LR input and the aligned latent.

  • Locally, tiles with low predicted error keep the aligned restoration, and the remaining tiles restart from the encoded bicubic-upsampled observation. Both sources share one denoising step, so restarts add no denoiser evaluation.
  • Globally, the full state resets when unreliable tiles cover at least half of the frame and a global LR appearance change is detected. The reset also clears the temporal conditions and the flow warm start, and it needs no future frames.
  • Disabling reliability control raises LPIPS at 29 cut events from 0.0820 to 0.1088, so the benefit comes from full resets.
03

Learning recurrent refinement

The backbone is trained for this formulation within a fixed per-frame budget, in four stages: spatial adaptation, one-step distillation, self-rollout fine-tuning and temporal fine-tuning.

  • Spatial adaptation teaches the U-Net to refine re-noised propagated estimates with spatially varying corruption, and one-step distillation compresses within-frame refinement from a shared initial latent.
  • Self-rollout fine-tuning replays detached, model-generated streaming states, including restarts, resets and cached decoder features, against the frozen initial student, and alternately updates the denoiser, adapter and temporal decoder. A flow-masked consistency loss matches temporal residuals to the ground truth, and a no-reference quality term adds MUSIQ and CLIP-IQA.
  • Temporal fine-tuning then adds a loss that matches the magnitude of inter-frame perceptual change to the ground truth within shots. On REDS4 it lowers tLP from 0.682 to 0.301.
04

Lightweight temporal modules

Since the initial latent already carries restored content, temporal guidance can focus on refining it. A ControlNet-style encoder that re-extracts features from the previous RGB output costs a great deal relative to one-step denoising, so ReLoopVSR uses a compact adapter and cached decoder features instead.

  • The convolutional temporal adapter (25.7M parameters) takes the denoiser input, the warped previous output and the DEN map, and produces 13 residuals. A gate predicted from the DEN map attenuates guidance in unreliable regions, and the adapter is bypassed on the first frame and at cuts.
  • The temporal decoder aligns and fuses cached block features through zero-initialized residuals, which avoids re-extraction from the previous RGB output and limits high-resolution cost.

ReLoopVSR-fast

Keyframe-sparse streaming inference

Adjacent frames are temporally redundant, so most frames can be updated from the recurrent state without a new diffusion step. In the spirit of SlowFast networks, ReLoopVSR-fast applies one-step diffusion only to keyframes and restores the frames in between with a lightweight fast decoder that uses the current LR input and gated, aligned history. It remains causal and emits each frame at once.

  • Keyframes begin with the first frame and recur at a nominal interval K; LR appearance changes trigger earlier keyframes, and every keyframe uses reliability control. K=0 denotes ReLoopVSR-base.
  • A larger K amortizes the denoiser over more frames at some cost in fidelity. Training follows the base recipe, plus backpropagation through time (BPTT) for the fast decoder.

FAQ

Questions and limits

What is ReLoopVSR?

ReLoopVSR is a compact recurrent autoregressive diffusion framework for streaming ×4 video super-resolution. It aligns and re-noises the previous denoised latent to initialize the current update and refines it with the current low-resolution observation through a shared one-step denoiser. Lightweight temporal modules reduce conditioning and decoding costs.

Local and global reliability control stops the propagation where errors are predicted, and rollout training with perceptual temporal supervision adapts the model to correct inherited errors. ReLoopVSR-fast runs the denoiser only on keyframes and restores the other frames with a lightweight fast decoder.

Why autoregressive?

Within a scene, the previous restoration, after motion compensation, already estimates the current frame, so correcting it is cheaper than generating the frame again from noise. Each update depends only on inputs that have already arrived, so no future-frame buffering is needed.

Repeated updates with the same denoiser on temporally corresponding content extend the effective computational depth across frames, with fixed parameters and one denoising step per frame.

What are ReLoopVSR-base and ReLoopVSR-fast?

ReLoopVSR-base runs one denoising step on every frame and offers the best fidelity. ReLoopVSR-fast runs the denoiser only on keyframes, at a nominal interval K from 4 to 20, and restores the frames in between with a 3.70M-parameter fast decoder. On REDS4, K=4 gives 85.7 FPS and K=20 gives 249.4 FPS at 720p in half precision, at some cost in fidelity.

How are scene cuts handled?

The full state resets when unreliable tiles cover at least half of the frame and a global LR appearance change is detected through a large color-histogram distance or low thumbnail correlation with the previous frame. The latent, the temporal conditions and the flow warm start are cleared together, using no future frames.

All 121 cuts in the full-film Tears of Steel streams are detected, and 29 of 34 cut pairs on LongAIGC-4. Strong lighting flashes can also trigger resets.

How does it compare with other methods?

ReLoopVSR achieves the lowest LPIPS and DISTS on all six paired benchmarks, the lowest tOF on all six and the lowest tLP on five. Under the common 720p protocol it depends on no future frames and has the shortest time to first frame and the lowest peak memory among the measured methods.

However, it does not lead every metric. Larger diffusion priors lead MUSIQ on every paired set, DOVE leads SSIM and VMAF on Vid4 and SPMCS, and bicubic upsampling itself tops SSIM on LongAIGC-8.

Is it real-time?

ReLoopVSR-fast exceeds 240 FPS at 720p and 28 FPS at 4K in half precision on one RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell. ReLoopVSR-base runs at 17.4 FPS at 720p in FP16 and at 3.58 FPS in strict FP32. Both are streaming in the causal sense, because each output is available without waiting for future frames.

How does it perform on real-world video?

With a checkpoint fine-tuned on compressed DOVE degradations, ReLoopVSR leads in MUSIQ, CLIP-IQA, MANIQA and BRISQUE on VideoLQ but trails four baselines in DOVER. VideoLQ has no ground truth, so these are no-reference scores and fidelity remains unmeasured.

What are the limits?

The evaluation covers only fixed ×4 restoration. The first frame and the frames right after a cut have no recurrent state, although quality recovers within a few frames. Fine periodic or aliased structures can be smoothed or replaced by a wrong pattern.

On SPMCS, DOVE leads several metrics and ReLoopVSR does not lead tLP.

Are the examples on this page representative?

The clips were chosen to be easy to read, so the benchmark tables are the appropriate reference for performance. Each clip is at the resolution stated in its caption.

How much GPU memory does it need? Does it run out of memory on long videos?

At 720p, peak allocation is 4.92 GiB for ReLoopVSR in strict FP32, 4.4 GiB for ReLoopVSR-base in FP16, and 2.2 GiB for ReLoopVSR-fast. In half precision, ReLoopVSR restores 4K video with 11.6 GiB of peak memory. The state has a fixed size and frames are processed one at a time, so memory stays constant with stream length.

Which scale factors are supported?

The evaluated model restores ×4 only, and other scale factors remain unevaluated.

Why might results look close to bicubic on my own video?

The default model is trained for bicubic ×4 inputs. Compressed or otherwise degraded video needs the real-world fine-tuned checkpoint, which is the version used for VideoLQ in the table above. Even then, gains depend on content and metric. Check also that the input is the low-resolution source rather than an already upscaled video.
